Earthquakes Don't Discriminate

From 1960 to 2026, over 2,600 significant earthquakes struck across the globe. They hit rich and poor countries alike.

Some earthquakes are enormous — magnitude 8 or 9. Others are moderate but still destructive. Size the dots by magnitude to see the variation.

But when we color by death toll, a different pattern emerges. The deadliest earthquakes aren't always the biggest.

The Ring of Fire around the Pacific hosts the most seismic activity. But within this ring, outcomes vary enormously by country.

The countries' wealth is related to earthquake mortality

We can see several predictors of deaths during earthquakes - including GDP, HDI (Human Development Index), and Hospital Beds. Let's look at all the factors together.

Each small chart plots one indicator against deaths per event. GDP, HDI, and hospital beds all show strong negative correlations - the r values are displayed on each chart.

Earthquakes are natural.
Disasters are not.

Across 66 years and 2,679 earthquakes, the data tells one consistent story: the countries that suffer most are not those with the strongest quakes, but those with the fewest resources to prepare and respond.

GDP per capita, human development, and healthcare infrastructure are stronger predictors of earthquake deaths than magnitude itself. The fault lines that kill are economic, not geological.

Methodology & Data

Earthquake data: NOAA NCEI Significant Earthquake Database (1960–2026, 2,679 events)

Socioeconomic data: World Bank Open Data (GDP, population density, urbanization, hospital beds)

Human Development Index: UNDP Human Development Reports
